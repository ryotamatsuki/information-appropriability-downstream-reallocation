# Obvious-Synthesis Test — Stage 6-J

## Proposed hostile synthesis

The strongest literature-based attack is:

1. **Acemoglu et al. (2022):** endogenous purchase selection changes the types who generate reviews and therefore the learning process.
2. **Chen–Du–Lei:** price interacts with review selection bias and may be adjusted to make reviews more informative.
3. **Yan et al. (2026):** review volume and valence determine learning precision; future quality refinement changes optimal introductory pricing and can make price move in nonstandard directions.
4. **Vertical-information literature:** downstream information can affect upstream quality/pricing and hence downstream payoffs.

This makes the institutional story highly crowded.

## Does this synthesis literally derive B-P1?

**Not cleanly from one published proposition.** Accessible model/proposition evidence did not reveal the exact parameter `ρ`, the exact fixed-likelihood benchmark, or the pair

\[
\frac{dc^*}{d\rho}>0,\qquad \frac{dc_F^*}{d\rho}<0.
\]

Therefore the literature-combination test alone is not recorded as an exact-prior kill.

## Algebraic synthesis

Once Stage 4-J is reduced to

\[
\max_c\; u(c)+\rho I(c),
\]

with

\[
I(c)=n(c)J(c),\quad n'(c)<0,
\]

the comparative static is governed by `I'(c)`. If selection improves conditional information quality sufficiently that

\[
n'(c)J(c)+n(c)J'(c)>0,
\]

then a larger weight on continuation value raises screening. If `J` is fixed, the derivative is negative. This is an elementary quantity-quality weighting argument.

## Verdict

**OBVIOUS-SYNTHESIS RISK: HIGH.**

But the canonical NO-GO does not need to assert that Acemoglu + Chen–Du–Lei + Yan formally prove B-P1. The stronger deterministic quantity-quality construction reproduces the theorem exactly and is sufficient for the kill.
