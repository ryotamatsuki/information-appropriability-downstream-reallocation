# Model-Level Prior-Art Audit

Search cutoff: **2026-09-03**.

Serious papers retained after deduplication: **38**.

Model/proposition-level or sufficiently detailed inspections: **20**.

The audit is not intended as exhaustive bibliography. It identifies the strongest model-level threats needed for Stage 3 routing.

## K1 — posterior allocation × downstream information investment

Strong threats:

1. Bonatti & Hörner (2017), *Career concerns with exponential learning*, Theoretical Economics — effort changes breakthrough probability; assessed ability determines wages; effort across time is strategically linked. **FULL MODEL / PROPOSITION VERIFIED.**
2. Cisternas (2018), *Career Concerns and the Nature of Skills*, AEJ Micro — endogenous effort, learning, market inference, dynamic incentives. **MODEL VERIFIED.**
3. Hörner & Lambert (2021), *Motivational Ratings*, Review of Economic Studies — ratings simultaneously provide information and motivate effort. **MODEL/PROPOSITION VERIFIED.**
4. Achim (2024), *Innovation through competitive experimentation*, Journal of Mathematical Economics — principal designs a dynamic contest for unobservable experimentation effort. **MODEL VERIFIED.**
5. Dewatripont, Jewitt & Tirole (1999), career-concerns information-structure papers. **CANONICAL PRIOR FAMILY.**

Disposition: component literature is mature, but the binding kill is stronger: posterior allocation can be replaced by a contest score/prize schedule.

## K2 — posterior allocation × strategic reporting/disclosure

Strong threats:

1. Klein & Mylovanov (2017), *Will truth out?—An advisor's quest to appear competent*, Journal of Mathematical Economics — dynamic reputational reporting incentives. **MODEL/PROPOSITION VERIFIED.**
2. Argenziano, Severinov & Squintani (2016), *Strategic Information Acquisition and Transmission*, AEJ Micro — costly information acquisition plus strategic communication; organization design comparisons. **MODEL/PROPOSITION VERIFIED.**
3. Henry & Ottaviani (2019), *Research and the Approval Process: The Organization of Persuasion*, AER — costly research, persuasion, approval authority and organization. **MODEL VERIFIED.**
4. “When second opinions hurt” (2012), JEBO — reputational cheap talk, interim information and endogenous verifiability. **MODEL VERIFIED.**
5. Ivanov (2010), *Informational control and organizational design*, JET. **MODEL VERIFIED.**

Disposition: **KILL — EXACT/WHOLE-GAME PRIOR ART FAMILY.** A future-allocation/reputation variant would require a proposition-level novelty argument before minimal modeling; Stage 3-K finds no distinct equilibrium topology.

## K3 — upstream action changes future signal technology

Strong threats:

1. Bolton & Harris (1999), *Strategic Experimentation*, Econometrica — multi-agent experimentation and dynamic information externalities. **FULL MODEL VERIFIED.**
2. Keller & Rady (2015), *Breakdowns*, Theoretical Economics — belief-dependent strategic experimentation with endogenous stopping. **FULL MODEL / PROPOSITION VERIFIED.**
3. Battigalli et al. (2019), *Learning and self-confirming long-run biases*, JET — endogenous information generation through recurrent actions/active experimentation. **MODEL VERIFIED.**
4. Filstroff et al. (2024), *Targeted Active Learning for Bayesian Decision-Making* — action/acquisition choice targets downstream decisions. **MODEL VERIFIED; adjacent field.**
5. Ambrus, Zhao & Zhi (2026), *Strategic Feedback Mechanisms and Experimentation* — information designer affects experimentation and purchase-generated signals. **WORKING PAPER VERIFIED.**
6. Keller & Rady / bandit lineage on controlled experimentation. **CANONICAL PRIOR FAMILY.**

Disposition: no single exact K3 paper is required for the kill. The candidate fails strong deterministic controlled-state replacement and minimal-player compression.

## K4 — belief-dependent participation × strategic data generation

Strong threats:

1. Farboodi, Mihet, Philippon & Veldkamp (2019), *Big Data and Firm Dynamics*, AEA P&P — firm activity generates data, data affects future performance and experimentation. **MODEL VERIFIED.**
2. Hagiu & Wright (2023), *Data-enabled learning, network effects, and competitive advantage*, RAND — customer data improves product quality and feeds back into future competition. **FULL MODEL VERIFIED.**
3. Laiho & Salmi (2026), *Coasian dynamics and endogenous learning*, JET — seller pricing and buyer participation interact with purchase-generated learning. **MODEL/PROPOSITION VERIFIED.**
4. Gieczewski & Kosterina (2024), *Experimentation in Endogenous Organizations*, Review of Economic Studies — beliefs from experimentation change organizational membership; membership changes experimentation environment. **MODEL VERIFIED.**
5. Farboodi & Veldkamp (2023), *Data and Markets*, Annual Review of Economics. **SURVEY VERIFIED.**
6. Baley & Veldkamp (2026), *Bayesian Learning* survey — includes data-economy feedback and active learning. **WORKING PAPER/SURVEY VERIFIED.**

Disposition: **KILL — STRONG GENERIC REPLACEMENT.** Data-feedback topology survives when data is a productive/capability stock.

## K5 — delegation/authority × information production

Strong threats:

1. Aghion & Tirole (1997), *Formal and Real Authority in Organizations*, JPE — allocation of formal authority changes information/initiative; information determines real authority. **FULL MODEL VERIFIED.**
2. Deimen & Szalay (2019), *Delegated Expertise, Authority, and Communication*, AER — expert chooses information under delegation versus communication. **FULL MODEL / PROPOSITION VERIFIED.**
3. Guo (2016), *Dynamic Delegation of Experimentation*, AER — delegation policy defined in belief space in a dynamic experimentation environment. **FULL MODEL VERIFIED.**
4. Escobar & Zhang (2021), *Delegating learning*, Theoretical Economics — principal dynamically delegates learning to an agent with evolving private information. **FULL MODEL VERIFIED.**
5. Ivanov (2010), *Informational control and organizational design*, JET. **MODEL VERIFIED.**
6. “Disagreement and informal delegation in organizations” (2021) — conditional delegation affects agent information acquisition effort. **MODEL VERIFIED.**

Disposition: **KILL — EXACT/WHOLE-GAME PRIOR ART FAMILY.** The proposed authority↔information loop is already central to the literature.

## K6 — integration/channel organization × information production

Strong threats:

1. Boot, Milbourn & Thakor (2000), *Boundaries of the Firm: A Theory of Informational Uncertainty and Learning* — firm boundaries are dynamically redrawn as the firm learns about uncertain opportunities/capabilities. **WORKING PAPER FULL MODEL AVAILABLE.**
2. Anderson & Parker (2002), *The Effect of Learning on the Make/Buy Decision* — learning creates path-dependent outsourcing traps and mixed sourcing regimes. **MODEL VERIFIED.**
3. Sorenson (2003), *Interdependence and Adaptability: Organizational Learning and the Long-Term Effect of Integration*, Management Science — integration changes learning/adaptability. **MODEL/EMPIRICAL THEORY VERIFIED.**
4. Baccara (2007), *Outsourcing, information leakage, and consulting firms*, RAND — outsourcing, information leakage and R&D incentives. **MODEL VERIFIED.**
5. Choi (1998), *Information concealment in the theory of vertical integration*, JEBO — vertical integration changes information observability. **MODEL VERIFIED.**
6. Gieczewski & Kosterina (2024), endogenous organizations and experimentation. **MODEL VERIFIED.**
7. Information uncertainty and organizational design (JAE 2022) — organizational structure responds empirically to information uncertainty. **EMPIRICAL SUPPORT.**

Disposition: no verified exact prior contains the precise K6 loop with strategic downstream information effort and repeated organization choice. Nevertheless K6 fails before Stage 4 because a deterministic organization-specific investment/capability model recreates the intended trap/reversal, and the minimal downstream effort response is compressible.

## K7 — competing experiments × posterior-based future rents

Strong threats:

1. Bolton & Harris (1999), *Strategic Experimentation*, Econometrica. **FULL MODEL VERIFIED.**
2. Thomas (2021), *Strategic Experimentation with Congestion*, AEJ Micro — competition adds strategic option value and changes experimentation incentives. **FULL MODEL VERIFIED.**
3. Achim (2024), *Innovation through competitive experimentation* — principal-agent contest for experimentation. **MODEL VERIFIED.**
4. Keller, Rady & Cripps / Keller–Rady strategic bandit lineage. **CANONICAL PRIOR FAMILY.**
5. Khalil, Lawarrée & Rodivilov (2026), *Rewarding success and failure: Moral hazard and adverse selection in strategic experimentation*, GEB — principal-agent experimentation with hidden effort and types. **MODEL VERIFIED.**

Disposition: **KILL — STRONG GENERIC REPLACEMENT.** The proposed future-rent logic maps to an R&D/innovation contest.

## K8 — reliability/reputation learning × endogenous reliance

Strong threats:

1. Lukyanov & Vlasova (2026), *Endogenous Vindication: Reputation and Effort in Expert Advice* — expert reputation changes client project/effort; client effort changes performance-test informativeness; expert advice determines exposure/testing; reputation then evolves. **WORKING PAPER FULL MODEL / PROPOSITION VERIFIED.** This is the strongest single-paper threat in Stage 3-K.
2. Bonatti & Hörner (2017), career concerns with coarse breakthrough learning. **FULL MODEL VERIFIED.**
3. Halac & Kremer (2020), *Experimenting with Career Concerns*, AEJ Micro — learning and reputation distort experimentation decisions. **FULL MODEL VERIFIED.**
4. Klein & Mylovanov (2017), dynamic reputational reporting. **MODEL VERIFIED.**
5. “When second opinions hurt” (2012), reputational expert advice with endogenous verification. **MODEL VERIFIED.**
6. Motivational ratings (Hörner & Lambert, 2021) — information/reputation system designed to motivate effort. **MODEL VERIFIED.**

Disposition: **KILL — EXACT/WHOLE-GAME PRIOR ART.** K8's defining belief→client action→test precision→belief feedback is already extremely close to Lukyanov–Vlasova.

## Overall finding

No candidate is killed merely because components are familiar. The candidates that pass generic/player/belief gates (K2, K5, K8) face direct mechanism-level prior art. The candidates with less direct prior art (K3, K6) fail the stronger generic and compression gates. There is therefore no Stage 4 candidate.
