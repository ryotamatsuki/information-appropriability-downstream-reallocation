# Stage 6-R — Equation Mapping

## 1. Frozen current model

Private objective:

`pi_S(e,eta) = m_S omega_S(eta) D_S(g(e)) - C(e)`.

Private FOC:

`m_S omega_S D_S'(x) g'(e) = C'(e)`.

Under concavity,

`sign(deP/deta) = sign(m_S omega_S' D_S' g') < 0` because `omega_S'<0`.

Coordinated objective:

`J = A_S omega_S D_S(g(e)) + A_R omega_R D_R(g(e)) - C(e)`.

Coordinated FOC:

`[A_S omega_S D_S' + A_R omega_R D_R'] g' = C'`.

IFT:

`deC/deta = - J_eeta / J_ee`.

Under concavity, the sign is the sign of

`A_S omega_S' D_S' + A_R omega_R' D_R'`.

Hence condition (G),

`A_R omega_R' D_R' > - A_S omega_S' D_S'`,

is exactly the positivity condition for the coordinated mixed partial.

For one-for-one reallocation with equal route response:

`omega_S=1-eta`, `omega_R=eta`, `D_S'=D_R'=D'`,

so the coordinated sign condition reduces to

`A_R > A_S`.

In the linear-quadratic welfare benchmark:

`deP/deta = -m beta/k < 0`,

`deC/deta = beta(c_S-c_R)/k > 0` when `c_S>c_R`.

## 2. Pu, Gong & Han (2017)

Publisher-verified centralized objective:

`Pi_C = (p_r-c)[a_r-beta_1 p_r + eps p_d + (1-tau)s] + (p_d-c)[a_d-beta_2 p_d + eps p_r + tau s] - eta_s s^2/2`.

The centralized decision variables are `p_r`, `p_d`, and `s`.

The effort FOC conditional on prices is

`eta_s s = (p_r-c)(1-tau) + (p_d-c)tau`.

A **partial** derivative holding prices fixed gives

`partial s / partial tau | prices = (p_d-p_r)/eta_s`.

This is not the full equilibrium comparative static because `p_r` and `p_d` are endogenous. The total derivative is

`eta_s ds/dtau = (p_d-p_r) + (1-tau) dp_r/dtau + tau dp_d/dtau`.

Pu et al. report for their deterministic baseline that both decentralized and centralized sales effort decrease as the number/share of free riders rises. Their economic setting is showrooming with purchase online at a lower price. Stage 6-R therefore does **not** certify `p_d>p_r` as an admissible parameter reversal inside the published baseline.

### Pu decentralized effort

The offline retailer is the effort provider and loses effort-generated purchases to the direct channel. The publisher's reported analytical result is `ds_D/dtau < 0` in the deterministic model. Exact closed-form retailer FOC was not needed for the kill because the paper itself reports the signed comparative static; evidence maturity is recorded as publisher model/result verified but not equation-by-equation appendix reproduction.

### Pu verdict

Pu establishes the same effort-spillover architecture and private decline, but **not** the required joint sign reversal in its published baseline. Classification: `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE`, not an independent immediate-corollary kill.

## 3. Xu, Tang, Lin & Lu (2022)

Author-uploaded full text and publisher metadata verify a dual-channel model in which offline retailer effort `e` creates demand in both channels. The demand terms include:

- offline effort-created demand: `(alpha-beta)e`;
- online effort-created demand: `beta e`;
- admissible free-riding range: `0 <= beta <= alpha`.

Thus, with normalization `eta_current = beta/alpha`, the effort-benefit allocation is exactly

`alpha(1-eta_current)e` offline and `alpha eta_current e` online.

This is the same one-for-one effort-benefit transfer used in the frozen benchmark, up to scale and the fact that Xu et al. endogenize additional prices/financial terms.

The full text reports in its numerical sensitivity analysis:

- decentralized effort falls as `beta` rises;
- centralized effort rises as `beta` rises when consumers prefer the online channel;
- centralized effort falls as `beta` rises when consumers prefer the offline channel.

The figures explicitly compare an offline-preference case (`theta=0.62`) and online-preference case (`theta=0.35`), with `theta` defined as offline-channel preference share.

Therefore an admissible model region exists in published prior art with the exact qualitative joint pattern

`deD/dbeta < 0 < deC/dbeta`.

This result is reported as model sensitivity rather than a general analytic theorem in `beta`. That distinction matters for wording but does not restore the current paper's economic novelty: the frozen theorem adds a generic mixed-partial condition (G) for a qualitative pattern already generated by the prior effort-reallocation game.

## 4. Label-stripped common form

Let a provider choose effort `e`, cost `C(e)`, and let parameter `z` move the fraction of effort-created value from the provider's route to another route.

Private:

`V_P = M_S(z) B_S(e) - C(e)`.

Integrated:

`V_I = M_S(z) B_S(e) + M_R(z) B_R(e) - C(e)`.

The current model is exactly this form.

Pu and Xu use more detailed demand/pricing systems but implement the same economic wedge: the effort provider bears the cost, free-riding reallocates some effort-created return to another channel, and integration internalizes both channels.

## 5. Condition (G) classification

Classification: **C/D — local mixed-partial sign condition / assumption equivalent to the desired coordinated sign under the stated concavity conditions.**

P3 proves that, once the private sign is generated by `omega_S'<0`, coordinated effort has the opposite sign exactly when the coordinated marginal value of effort rises with reallocation. The one-for-one reduction `A_R>A_S` gives economic language to that condition but does not introduce a new strategic feedback.

## 6. Immediate-corollary decision

- Relative to **Pu 2017 alone**: `NO` — the Stage-11 price-holding argument is not a verified full-equilibrium immediate corollary in an admissible published region.
- Relative to the **dual-channel free-riding literature as a whole**: the qualitative P3 sign pair is already generated by Xu et al. 2022, and the current generalization adds no new strategic primitive. The current claim is therefore contribution-grade absorbed even though the exact general IFT statement is not literally a prior theorem.

For the required categorical ledger, P3 is recorded as `STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE` at literal theorem level, with **no independent contribution surviving** because its additional generality is functional-form/sign-characterization generality rather than new economics.
