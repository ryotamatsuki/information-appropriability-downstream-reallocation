# Stage 4-J Targeted Prior-Art Audit

Search cutoff: 2026-09-03. This is a Stage 4 targeted escalation, not a Stage 6 novelty certification.

## Actual derived Stage 4-J result being compared

The exact Stage 4-J result is not merely that selection changes reviews. In the canonical model:

1. a distinct downstream retailer chooses a commercial screening cutoff `c`;
2. the cutoff changes the latent purchaser mixture;
3. the manufacturer observes the cutoff but not purchaser type and therefore faces a `c`-dependent Bayesian experiment;
4. manufacturer adaptation creates continuation value for the retailer;
5. increasing the retailer's continuation stake raises equilibrium screening (`dc*/drho>0`);
6. in an otherwise identical fixed-likelihood benchmark that preserves feedback volume, the sign reverses (`dc_F*/drho<0`);
7. the positive sign is impossible when purchaser type is observed or when information is replaced by nonnegative additive deterministic output.

## Strongest threats

### 1. Chen, Du & Lei — *The Interactions of Customer Reviews and Price and Their Dual Roles in Conveying Quality Information* (Marketing Science, online 2024; 44(1), 2025)

**Classification: STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE.**

The paper directly analyzes uncertain product quality, customer reviews, price, and review selection bias. Its published abstract states that price can be adjusted to mitigate review selection bias and make reviews more informative. This is the strongest threat to the `screening -> informativeness` part of Stage 4-J.

However, the inspected evidence indicates a seller with private-quality signaling incentives and consumers learning quality. Stage 4-J instead has an uninformed downstream retailer, a separate upstream Bayesian decision maker, and a post-feedback adaptation action whose value feeds back into retailer screening. The Stage 4-J B-P1 comparative static in the retailer's continuation stake, together with the fixed-likelihood sign reversal, was not verified in this prior.

Direct relabeling to B-P1: `PARTIAL`, not `YES`.

### 2. Acemoglu, Makhdoumi, Malekian & Ozdaglar (2022), *Learning From Reviews: The Selection Effect and the Speed of Learning*, Econometrica 90(6):2857–2899

**Classification: COMPONENT OVERLAP / STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE ON THE STATISTICAL SELECTION OBJECT.**

This is the canonical selection-effect prior. Consumers know ex-ante taste, purchase decisions select customer types, and the distribution of subsequent reviews depends on the selected composition. The model is fully Bayesian and explicitly studies how selection changes learning speed.

But purchase selection is generated by customer beliefs/tastes rather than a strategic downstream intermediary maximizing a continuation return from a separate upstream adaptation choice. No inspected proposition reproduces `dc*/drho>0` versus `dc_F*/drho<0`.

Direct relabeling to the full Stage 4-J game: `NO`.

### 3. Yan, Bian, Perera & Guan (2026), *Dynamic Pricing and Product Quality with Review-Driven Learning*, Production and Operations Management 35(7):2659–2681

**Classification: STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE.**

This recent paper is a serious supply-side threat. A firm jointly considers dynamic pricing and post-launch quality refinement while learning from early reviews. The published abstract reports that review volume and valence affect learning precision and that the optimal introductory price may move in either direction when quality refinement is available.

The key difference is organizational and informational: the inspected evidence describes an integrated firm rather than a downstream retailer choosing a latent-mixture screening rule for a separate upstream Bayesian adapter. The specific selected-sample likelihood derivative and fixed-likelihood sign-reversal theorem of B-P1 were not verified.

Direct relabeling: `PARTIAL`.

### 4. Hu, Sun, Zheng, Chen & Huang (2021), *Committed or contingent? The retailer's information acquisition and sharing strategies when confronting manufacturer encroachment*, IJPE 242:108294

**Classification: COMPONENT OVERLAP / STRUCTURALLY VERY CLOSE ON VERTICAL INCENTIVES.**

A retailer strategically acquires consumer quality-preference information; the manufacturer chooses product quality, and acquisition timing affects quality incentives. This is the strongest vertical-IO threat.

But information is acquired as a discrete/strategic information-management action, not generated by ordinary commercial customer screening through a selected latent mixture. The B-P1 sign reversal is not present in the inspected evidence.

Direct relabeling: `NO`.

### 5. Guan, Mantrala & Bian (2019), *Strategic Information Management in a Distribution Channel*, Journal of Retailing 95(1):42–56

**Classification: COMPONENT OVERLAP.**

The manufacturer and retailer possess different quality/preference information and choose costly information-gap-filling actions under alternative timing structures. It is relevant to vertical information incentives, but not to endogenous selected-customer likelihood.

### 6. Wang et al. (2019), *Managing a Supply Chain under the Impact of Customer Reviews: A Two-Period Game Analysis*, EJOR 277(2):454–468

**Classification: COMPONENT OVERLAP.**

Two-period manufacturer-retailer pricing/quality decisions interact with customer reviews. The inspected abstract does not establish the latent-type selected-likelihood mechanism or B-P1.

## Immediate-corollary test

The targeted evidence creates a serious warning but does not yet produce an immediate exact corollary of one prior theorem.

- Acemoglu et al. supplies the selected-review likelihood logic.
- Chen–Du–Lei supplies endogenous price adjustment to mitigate review-selection bias.
- Yan et al. supplies review-driven quality adaptation and ambiguous introductory pricing.
- Hu et al. supplies vertical retailer-information/manufacturer-quality incentives.

Combining these ingredients makes Stage 6 novelty risk high. But Stage 4-J's exact equilibrium comparative static is generated by one minimal full game and survives the ex-ante benchmark kills. No single inspected prior was verified to deliver the same `continuation stake -> retailer screening` sign reversal against a fixed-likelihood volume benchmark.

**Stage 4 immediate-corollary verdict: NOT KILLED, BUT HIGH NOVELTY RISK.**

## Stage 4 prior-art conclusion

No exact/whole-game absorption was verified at Stage 4. The strongest targeted threat is Chen–Du–Lei, followed closely by Yan et al. for supply-side review-driven quality adjustment and Acemoglu et al. for the core selection effect.

Accordingly, prior art does not overturn the mathematical GO at Stage 4, but any GO must route directly to a hostile Stage 6 proposition-level novelty re-kill focused especially on whether B-P1 is an obvious synthesis of these three lines.
