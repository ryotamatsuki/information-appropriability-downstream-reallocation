# Stage 4-J Decision Log

## D1 — Keep exactly one information-specific object

Decision: keep only the selected-sample likelihood generated by ordinary commercial screening. No separate information effort, disclosure, attention, contracts, or second retailer was added.

## D2 — Use one consumer rather than a continuum

Decision: one potential consumer with `z~U[0,1]` is sufficient. Purchase probability `1-c` and selected-type mixture are well defined without introducing market-size primitives.

## D3 — Manufacturer does not observe purchaser type

Decision: `M` observes the screening rule and feedback but not `z`. This is not an irrationality assumption; `M` knows the induced mixture and updates correctly. The type-observed benchmark is solved separately and kills the positive composition effect.

## D4 — Continuous quadratic adaptation

Decision: use `a*=posterior mean` under quadratic loss. This gives an exact Bayes-risk value and avoids threshold artifacts.

## D5 — Do not introduce project-level eta

Decision: `eta` is omitted. The core selected-likelihood mechanism survives without it, and adding route share would violate the instruction not to reconnect the project title before the mechanism passes Stage 4.

## D6 — Primary analytical family

Decision: retain the unrestricted affine feedback model for general identities and numerical audit, but prove B-P1 in the interior symmetric-crossing family

`p_0(z)=1/2+t-2tz`,

`p_1(z)=1/2-t+2tz`, `0<t<1/2`.

Reason: it makes the latent-mixture cancellation channel transparent and yields exact closed forms. It is not a boundary calibration: the exact witness uses feedback probabilities in `[1/5,4/5]`. Strict inequalities plus local uniqueness extend the result to an open neighborhood of unrestricted affine primitives.

## D7 — Strong fixed-likelihood benchmark

Decision: preserve purchase probability and feedback volume. Only freeze the conditional experiment value. This prevents a mechanical `effect versus no effect` comparison.

## D8 — Strong generic replacement

Decision: allow arbitrary nonnegative type-dependent deterministic productivity `v(z)`. The total additive output still satisfies `H'(c)=-v(c)<=0`. B-P1 therefore does not rely on comparing against a weak constant-output analogue.

## D9 — B-P2 downgraded

Decision: the natural secondary implication in the canonical family is `less feedback volume but greater total decision value` as continuation stakes increase. This is a direct corollary of B-P1, not an independent contribution. Do not revive the originally suggested opposite-volume direction.

## D10 — Welfare kept secondary

Decision: derive exact integrated screening but reject a universal retailer/planner ordering. Both monopoly screening and adaptation appropriation matter. B-W1 is conditional and secondary.

## D11 — Targeted prior-art escalation

Strongest threats after solving the actual model:

1. Chen–Du–Lei (Marketing Science 2024/2025): price may mitigate review-selection bias and make reviews more informative.
2. Yan–Bian–Perera–Guan (POM 2026): review-driven learning, dynamic pricing, and post-launch quality refinement; introductory price may move either way.
3. Acemoglu et al. (Econometrica 2022): canonical endogenous review-selection effect.
4. Hu et al. (IJPE 2021): retailer information acquisition interacting with manufacturer quality.

No single inspected prior was verified to reproduce the full B-P1 game and fixed-likelihood sign reversal. Nevertheless the combination threat is strong and must be the first issue in Stage 6.
