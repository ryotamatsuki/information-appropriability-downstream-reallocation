# Stage 4 Contract — Endogenous Customer-Selection Minimal Model Hard Kill

This contract is binding if Stage 3-J is merged. Stage 4 may test **only Candidate B**.

## Research question

Can a downstream retailer's ordinary commercial screening choice alter the statistical likelihood from which an upstream manufacturer learns, and thereby change the retailer's own equilibrium screening incentives through the manufacturer's subsequent Bayesian product adaptation?

## Players

Minimum only:

1. upstream manufacturer `M`;
2. downstream retailer/dealer `R`;
3. short-lived heterogeneous consumers as non-strategic Bayesian/data-generating agents conditional on private taste.

Do not add a second strategic retailer/direct seller unless the one-retailer architecture is formally incapable of expressing the mechanism.

## Timing

1. Nature draws binary product state `theta in {0,1}` from common prior `mu_0`.
2. `R` chooses one scalar first-period commercial screening action `c`, preferably price or an equivalent purchase cutoff.
3. Consumers with private taste `z ~ F` self-select into purchase according to the screening rule.
4. Selected purchasers generate feedback `y` with distribution depending on both `theta` and `z`.
5. `M` observes the screening rule and selected feedback, knows the selection mechanism, and updates rationally.
6. `M` chooses one adaptation action `a` based on the posterior.
7. Continuation payoffs are realized; `R` receives only a share of the value generated by correct/productive adaptation.

No disclosure game, contract design, bargaining, entry, ownership choice or behavioral inference may be added in the baseline.

## Information structure

Prior:

`Pr(theta=1)=mu_0`.

Consumer type:

`z ~ F(z)`.

Screening induces selected set/distribution `F_c(z)`.

Binary feedback is preferred for tractability:

`Pr(y=1 | theta,z) = p_theta(z)`

with parameters chosen so customer type changes informativeness without directly assuming the desired equilibrium result.

The manufacturer likelihood is

`ell_theta(y;c) = integral p_theta(y|z) dF_c(z)`.

Posterior:

`mu_1(c,y) = mu_0 ell_1(y;c) / [mu_0 ell_1(y;c) + (1-mu_0) ell_0(y;c)]`.

The selection derivative `d ell_theta(y;c)/dc` is the core information-specific object.

## Strategic actions

- `R`: one screening/commercial action `c`.
- `M`: one post-feedback adaptation action `a`.

Consumers only choose purchase based on the specified utility/cutoff rule. Their role must not create a separate strategic game.

## Payoff skeleton

Retailer:

`Pi_R(c) = current_margin(c) + delta * rho * E[V_R(a(mu_1(c,y)),theta) | c]`.

Manufacturer:

`Pi_M(a) = current/upstream terms + delta * E[V_M(a,theta) | mu_1]`.

Use the simplest adaptation loss/benefit that yields a well-defined posterior decision rule. Quadratic posterior-matching or a binary threshold action are both allowed; choose the one producing the cleanest exact comparative statics.

`rho` is the retailer's continuation appropriation share. It is not a novelty primitive; it exists only to create a vertical incentive wedge.

## Downstream reallocation parameter

The project-level reallocation parameter `eta` may be introduced only minimally, for example as an exogenous share of customer mass routed through `R` versus a passive/fixed-selection outside route. It must not duplicate `c` or mechanically change both information volume and appropriability at once.

Stage 4 should first solve the core `M-R-consumer` selected-likelihood feedback. Add `eta` only if it is necessary to reconnect the formal proposition to downstream reallocation without changing the mechanism family.

## Information-specific object

Exactly one:

`endogenous selected-sample likelihood induced by commercial customer screening`.

Do not add separate endogenous signal precision or information-acquisition effort.

## Full strategic loop

`R screening c`
`-> selected customer-type distribution F_c`
`-> feedback likelihood ell_theta(y;c)`
`-> M posterior mu_1`
`-> M adaptation a`
`-> R continuation payoff`
`-> R screening c`.

The headline theorem must have every arrow active.

## Generic replacement benchmark

Replace feedback/Bayesian learning by deterministic productive task output while retaining the same screening/current-demand and continuation-payoff structure.

If the Stage-4 headline proposition remains, return `NO-GO — GENERIC MULTI-TASK/OUTPUT REDUCTION`.

## Information-deletion benchmark

Hold the feedback likelihood fixed with respect to screening:

`ell_theta(y;c) = ell_theta(y)`.

Retain current demand and the post-feedback adaptation stage.

If the headline sign/threshold result survives unchanged, return `NO-GO — SELECTION LIKELIHOOD NOT ESSENTIAL`.

## Strategic-feedback-off benchmark

Set the retailer's continuation stake in upstream adaptation to zero, or otherwise remove the arrow `M adaptation -> R screening incentive`, while preserving selected-sample learning for the manufacturer.

If the main result remains as merely a learning-speed/selection result, return `NO-GO — NOT A VERTICAL STRATEGIC INTERACTION`.

## Closest prior model

Primary: Acemoglu, Makhdoumi, Malekian & Ozdaglar (2022), *Learning From Reviews: The Selection Effect and the Speed of Learning*.

Secondary vertical threats: Hu et al. (2021), Guan–Mantrala–Bian (2019), Shin & Zeevi (2024), Yan et al. (2026), Chen–Du–Lei (2024/2025), Wang et al. (2019).

Stage 4 must build a dimension-by-dimension mapping before calling any proposition novel.

## Candidate propositions — maximum three

### B-P1 — Primary hard-kill proposition

There exists a parameter region in which the equilibrium screening response to the retailer's continuation stake in correct upstream adaptation has a different sign or threshold under endogenous selected-feedback likelihood than under the otherwise identical fixed-likelihood benchmark.

The proof must explicitly use `d ell_theta(y;c)/dc`.

### B-P2 — Secondary only

There may exist a region in which equilibrium screening raises expected feedback volume while lowering expected posterior decision value because the customer composition becomes less informative. This is admissible only if it follows from the vertical equilibrium feedback and is not merely the known review-selection effect.

### B-W1 — Welfare

Compare decentralized retailer screening with integrated screening. Determine analytically, rather than assume, whether partial appropriation of adaptation value leads to excessive or insufficient screening/selectivity.

## Primary Stage-4 hard-kill test

Solve B-P1 in the full model and fixed-likelihood benchmark. If no qualitative difference exists, kill the architecture immediately and do not add features.

## Primary generic-replacement kill

Show that B-P1 cannot be reproduced after replacing Bayesian selected-feedback learning with deterministic production/task output. If it can, kill immediately.

## Stage-4 scope discipline

Stage 4 may simplify distributions and payoffs but may not introduce:

- information disclosure/garbling;
- contracting;
- firm-size heterogeneity;
- multiple strategic downstream firms;
- endogenous entry;
- additional information efforts;
- attention constraints;
- regime change;
- rare-event Poisson learning.

If B fails, record NO-GO. Do not activate A, C or D automatically.

## Required Stage-4 verdict

- `GO` only if a proved full-model information-specific proposition survives both benchmark kills and model-level closest-prior comparison.
- `CONDITIONAL GO` only for one exact formal deficiency.
- otherwise `NO-GO`.
