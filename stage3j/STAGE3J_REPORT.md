## Binding diagnosis from Stage 6-H

The next theorem must fail after replacing information by ordinary separable tasks/outputs. It must also be proved with the distinctive information-specific feedback active in the full model, not only in a benchmark where that interaction disappears.

## Four candidates

### A — Rare-Event / Field-Failure Learning

Information object: event/no-event Bayesian learning with state-dependent Poisson intensities.

Strongest prior threats: Keller & Rady (2015, *Breakdowns*), Keller & Rady (2010, *Strategic Experimentation with Poisson Bandits*), Bolton & Harris (1999), Das–Klein–Schmid (2020), and Xu & Yang (2026, *Dynamic Pricing With Recommendation and Consumer Feedback*).

The information object is genuine and survives generic task replacement. However, the proposed loop is too close to established strategic experimentation: endogenous activity changes Poisson learning rates, beliefs change continuation actions, and players internalize/free-ride on future information. Xu–Yang additionally gives an economically close consumer-feedback technology in which sales raise the Poisson feedback rate and good/bad/no-news histories change optimal commercial actions.

Verdict: `KILL — IMMEDIATE COROLLARY`.

### B — Endogenous Customer Selection / Endogenous Sampling

Information object: an endogenous likelihood generated by the types of customers selected into purchase/review.

The critical object is not review noise or precision chosen directly. A downstream commercial action changes the conditional type distribution of purchasers/reviewers, so it changes the likelihood ratio used for upstream Bayesian learning. The upstream adaptation decision in turn changes downstream continuation value, giving the downstream actor a strategic reason to change the selection rule.

Closest threats:

- Acemoglu, Makhdoumi, Malekian & Ozdaglar (2022): endogenous review-selection effect and learning speed.
- Hu, Sun, Zheng, Chen & Huang (2021): retailer information acquisition/sharing with manufacturer encroachment and endogenous manufacturer quality.
- Shin & Zeevi (2024): platform information sharing, manufacturer information acquisition/product quality, and reviews.
- Yan, Bian, Perera & Guan (2026): review-driven learning with endogenous pricing and post-launch product-quality refinement.
- Chen, Du & Lei (2025 issue / 2024 online): price responds strategically to review selection bias.
- Wang et al. (2019): two-period manufacturer–retailer game with customer reviews and endogenous quality/prices.

No inspected single model combines (i) a strategic downstream actor whose ordinary commercial action endogenously changes the sampling likelihood, (ii) rational upstream Bayesian updating conditional on that endogenous selection, and (iii) upstream adaptation whose continuation value feeds back into the downstream actor's initial selection action.

Generic task replacement destroys the object: with no inference problem there is no selection-dependent likelihood and no value of changing the composition of observations holding the physical action fixed.

Verdict: `PREFERRED`.

### C — Upstream Attention × Downstream Information Acquisition

Information object: downstream information production must be processed by scarce upstream attention.

Fatal prior: Chen & Suen (2023), *Competition for Attention and News Quality*, explicitly has receiver attention allocation depending on and feeding back into information providers' quality choices, including a downward spiral. Liang–Mu–Syrgkanis (2022), Lipnowski–Mathevet–Wei (2020), Argenziano–Severinov–Squintani (2016), and the principal–expert literature make the neighborhood still denser.

Verdict: `KILL — EXACT/WHOLE-GAME PRIOR ART` for the intended effort-attention feedback family.

### D — Information Obsolescence / Endogenous Regime Relevance

Information object: latent regime changes make legacy observations differently informative about the current state.

Strongest threat: Keller & Rady (1999), *Optimal Experimentation in a Changing Environment*, already studies endogenous experimentation when the unknown payoff environment switches randomly, with extreme/moderate experimentation regimes, tracking quality and information traps. Regime-learning and structural-break literatures provide the statistical component. To create a separate downstream strategic theorem would require at least a latent regime, strategic refresh/acquisition effort, and a vertical continuation action; minimal formulations found in this audit therefore breach the one-object/complexity discipline or collapse to standard changing-environment experimentation.

Verdict: `KILL — UNCONTROLLED COMPLEXITY`.

## Preferred B architecture

### Core research question

How does downstream commercial selection of customers distort the statistical sample from which an upstream manufacturer learns, when the downstream actor anticipates that upstream product adaptation changes its own continuation payoff?

### Minimum players

- upstream manufacturer `M`;
- downstream retailer/dealer `R`;
- short-lived consumers with heterogeneous private tastes.

### Minimum timing

1. prior over a binary payoff-relevant product state `theta` is common;
2. `R` chooses a first-period commercial screening action, preferably price/cutoff `c`;
3. consumers self-select into purchase according to `c` and their private taste `z`;
4. purchasers generate feedback with distribution depending jointly on `theta` and `z`;
5. `M`, observing the commercial rule and the selected feedback, updates rationally;
6. `M` chooses a product adaptation/action `a`;
7. continuation profits are realized, including `R`'s share of the adaptation benefit.

A downstream-reallocation parameter may scale which customer pool is exposed to the retailer versus a passive direct route, but Stage 4 must not add a second strategic downstream player unless the one-retailer model cannot express the mechanism.

### Information-specific feedback

`R commercial action -> selected customer-type distribution -> feedback likelihood -> M posterior -> M adaptation -> R continuation payoff -> R commercial action`.

### Primary Stage-4 hard-kill proposition

Show that the sign or threshold governing `R`'s first-period screening response to the value of upstream adaptation changes when the feedback likelihood is endogenous to customer selection, relative to an otherwise identical fixed-likelihood benchmark. The result must be unavailable if the selected-feedback likelihood is held fixed.

### Candidate secondary proposition

There may exist a region in which a commercial action increases the number of observed reviews but lowers expected posterior decision value because it changes the selected-customer likelihood; this may be retained only if it is an equilibrium implication of the vertical feedback rather than a restatement of Acemoglu et al.'s selection effect.

### Welfare candidate

Compare decentralized `R` screening with integrated-channel screening. Because `R` captures only part of the value of correct upstream adaptation, the decentralized selection rule may be too inclusive or too exclusive. Stage 4 must determine the sign; do not assume it.

## Generic replacement gate
