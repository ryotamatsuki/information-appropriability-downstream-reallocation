\title{Information Appropriability under Downstream Reallocation}
\author{}

\begin{abstract}
We study how downstream reallocation affects incentives to produce information when the information is generated at one downstream node but improves an upstream product used across routes. The information-producing node internalizes only the return tied to its own downstream footprint, whereas a coordinated system internalizes the value of reusable information through both the shrinking and expanding routes. In a general model, private information effort falls as activity reallocates away from the information producer. Coordinated effort instead rises when the expanding route's weighted marginal return to product-improvement information exceeds the return lost on the shrinking route. This yields a reallocation-induced divergence in private and coordinated information incentives. Cross-route reuse is necessary for the positive coordinated response in the separable model. A quasilinear welfare benchmark further delivers $e^{P}<e^{C}<e^{W}$ and shows that the private--coordinated and private--social effort gaps widen with downstream reallocation under the stated restrictions. The mechanism identifies an information-appropriability problem created by the changing location of downstream activity rather than by information externalities or channel structure per se.
\end{abstract}
