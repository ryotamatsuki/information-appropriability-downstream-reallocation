\section{Equilibrium Characterization}\label{sec:equilibrium}

We first characterize the effort chosen by the information-producing node and then the effort preferred by a vertically coordinated system. The comparison isolates the source of the appropriability wedge before imposing any parametric demand structure.

\subsection{Private information effort}

Differentiating \eqref{eq:privateobjective} with respect to effort gives the private first-order condition
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_P(e,\eta)
    \equiv
    m_S\omega_S(\eta)D_S'(g(e))g'(e)-C'(e)=0.
    \label{eq:privatefoc}
\end{equation}
Let $\Eprivate(\eta)$ denote the unique interior solution. We assume the private second-order condition
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_{P,e}(\Eprivate,\eta)<0.
    \label{eq:privatesoc}
\end{equation}
By the Implicit Function Theorem,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\Eprivate}{d\eta}
    =-
    \frac{m_S\omega_S'(\eta)D_S'(g(\Eprivate))g'(\Eprivate)}
    {\Phi_{P,e}(\Eprivate,\eta)}.
    \label{eq:privateift}
\end{equation}
Because $m_S>0$, $D_S'>0$, $g'>0$, $\omega_S'<0$, and the denominator is negative, the private effort response is negative. Reallocation reduces the number of own-route transactions over which $S$ can appropriate the return to its information effort.

\subsection{Coordinated information effort}

Differentiating \eqref{eq:coordinatedobjective} yields
\begin{align}
    \Phi_C(e,\eta)
    \equiv{}&
    \left[A_S\omega_S(\eta)D_S'(g(e))
    +A_R\omega_R(\eta)D_R'(g(e))\right]g'(e)
    -C'(e)=0.
    \label{eq:coordinatedfoc}
\end{align}
Let $\Ecoordinated(\eta)$ denote the unique interior coordinated solution and assume
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_{C,e}(\Ecoordinated,\eta)<0.
    \label{eq:coordinatedsoc}
\end{equation}
The Implicit Function Theorem gives
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\Ecoordinated}{d\eta}
    =-
    \frac{
    \left[A_S\omega_S'(\eta)D_S'(g(\Ecoordinated))
    +A_R\omega_R'(\eta)D_R'(g(\Ecoordinated))\right]g'(\Ecoordinated)
    }
    {\Phi_{C,e}(\Ecoordinated,\eta)}.
    \label{eq:coordinatedift}
\end{equation}
Unlike the private derivative, the numerator in \eqref{eq:coordinatedift} contains two reallocation effects. The first is negative: the system loses information value through the shrinking route $S$. The second is positive: the same information becomes more valuable through the expanding route $R$. Since the denominator is negative, the sign of the coordinated response is determined by the sign of the bracketed weighted marginal-return term.

\subsection{The appropriability wedge}

Equations \eqref{eq:privateift} and \eqref{eq:coordinatedift} make the distinction between private and coordinated incentives explicit. The private chooser sees only
\[
    m_S\omega_S'(\eta)D_S'(x),
\]
which is negative whenever the information producer's footprint shrinks. The coordinated system instead sees
\[
    A_S\omega_S'(\eta)D_S'(x)
    +A_R\omega_R'(\eta)D_R'(x),
\]
which can be positive because information is reusable through the expanding route. Thus the relevant inefficiency is not simply that information creates a positive externality. It is that downstream reallocation changes the relative weights on appropriable and nonappropriable returns to the same information input.

The next section states the exact condition under which the two responses have opposite signs.
