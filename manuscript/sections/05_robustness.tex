\section{Robustness and Scope}\label{sec:robustness}

The sign divergence in Theorem~\ref{thm:divergence} is not a consequence of the linear--quadratic welfare benchmark. The general formulation in Sections~\ref{sec:model}--\ref{sec:mainresults} already allows nonlinear route activity, nonlinear learning, and nonquadratic effort costs. What matters is the sign structure of the comparative statics, not the particular closed forms used in Section~\ref{sec:welfare}.

\subsection{Nonlinear route reallocation}

The route weights $\omega_S(\eta)$ and $\omega_R(\eta)$ need not be linear or sum to a constant. The private derivative in \eqref{eq:privateift} remains negative whenever the information producer's appropriable footprint is locally shrinking, $\omega_S'(\eta)<0$, and the private problem remains locally concave. For the coordinated problem, nonlinear route reallocation changes the magnitude of the two terms in condition~\eqref{eq:G} but not its economic content. The positive coordinated response requires the expanding route's weighted marginal return to dominate the loss on the shrinking route.

This observation also clarifies what the theorem does not require. It does not require a one-for-one transfer of activity between routes. Corollary~\ref{cor:oneforone} imposes one-for-one reallocation only to obtain the transparent reduction $A_R>A_S$.

\subsection{Nonlinear information production}

The learning technology $x=g(e)$ may be nonlinear. Equations \eqref{eq:privateift} and \eqref{eq:coordinatedift} retain $g'(e)$ explicitly, while curvature in $g$ enters the second-order conditions. Hence diminishing or increasing marginal information productivity is compatible with the result as long as $g'(e)>0$ in the relevant region and the effort problems have unique interior concave optima.

Similarly, the effort cost $C(e)$ need not be quadratic. Nonquadratic cost changes the equilibrium level of effort and the IFT denominator but does not change the sign logic when $C$ is sufficiently convex to maintain the required second-order conditions. The quadratic form in Section~\ref{sec:welfare} is used only to display the private, coordinated, and social effort levels in closed form.

\subsection{Demand curvature}

Route-specific responses $D_S(x)$ and $D_R(x)$ may also be nonlinear. Their first derivatives determine the marginal value of information in condition~\eqref{eq:G}, while their second derivatives affect local concavity. The theorem therefore permits heterogeneous information productivity across routes. Indeed, heterogeneity in $D_R'$ is economically meaningful: greater reuse value on the expanding route makes condition~\eqref{eq:G} easier to satisfy.

\subsection{A utility-based benchmark}

Section~\ref{sec:welfare} provides a separate utility microfoundation with quasilinear preferences, linear benchmark learning, and quadratic effort cost. Its role is to discipline welfare accounting and to distinguish private, coordinated, and social objectives. The ordering $\Eprivate<\Ecoordinated<\Esocial$ and the widening wedges are benchmark results under the stated parameter restrictions; they should not be read as unconditional implications of every member of the general model class.

\subsection{Boundary of robustness}

The theorem is therefore best described as \emph{robust under conditions}. Two conditions cannot be dispensed with. First, the private sign requires a shrinking appropriable footprint and the relevant concavity condition. Second, the positive coordinated sign requires condition~\eqref{eq:G}. Corollary~\ref{cor:reuse} shows that when information has no cross-route use, the coordinated sign reverses and the headline divergence disappears.

The robustness analysis does not introduce bargaining, strategic pricing, endogenous consumer sorting, dynamics, additional firms, or a separate information-revelation game. Such modifications could generate other economic forces, but they are outside the frozen theory and are not needed for the mechanism established here.
