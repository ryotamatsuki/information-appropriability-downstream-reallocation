# Claim Ledger

## ALLOWED

- Downstream reallocation can reduce an information producer's private effort while increasing coordinated information effort under condition (G).
- In the frozen welfare benchmark, private, coordinated, and social efforts satisfy `e^P<e^C<e^W`.
- In that benchmark, the private–system effort gaps widen with downstream reallocation.
- A local under-retention region can exist around a valid private retention threshold.
- The main mechanism is application-independent and can be described as information appropriability under downstream reallocation.

## ALLOWED WITH QUALIFICATION

- The main theorem is **robust under conditions**, not unconditional or fully general.
- Cross-industry evidence supports the primitive field/service-information → upstream-product-improvement chain; it does not prove the full theoretical mechanism.
- The exact dealer-rent → information-effort causal elasticity is a theoretical incentive primitive, not an established empirical fact.
- P1 may be used as a tractable supporting/robustness result, not as the central novelty claim.
- Closest-literature distinction must remain proposition-level and bounded by the Stage-6 audit.

## PROHIBITED

- claiming that downstream feedback, user innovation, costly information acquisition, positive information externalities, retailer information sharing, or endogenous channel structure are themselves new;
- claiming the Stage-2 RRC / foreclosure / wholesale-pricing mechanism as a contribution;
- presenting P4 hump-shaped retention as a general result;
- saying Panasonic or another institutional example proves the complete model;
- describing `η` as total market scale; it is a downstream-reallocation / route-prominence index;
- claiming the sign reversal is unconditional;
- using “first”, “unprecedented”, or equivalent broad priority claims without a new novelty gate.
