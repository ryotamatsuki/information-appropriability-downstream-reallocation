# Canonical Theory Freeze Candidate

## Research question

How does reallocating downstream activity away from an information-producing node affect private information production when that information improves an upstream product used across routes, and when can private and system-optimal information effort move in opposite directions?

## Economic problem

A downstream node produces information that improves an upstream product used across downstream routes. Reallocation can reduce the node's appropriable return while increasing the system-wide marginal value of the same reusable information.

## Mechanism label

**Information Appropriability under Downstream Reallocation**

## Core mechanism

**shrinking appropriable own-route return** versus **expanding cross-route system return to reusable information**.

## Main theorem

Under a shrinking producer footprint, incomplete appropriability of noncontractible information, cross-route product-improvement value, unique interior effort solutions, and condition (G), downstream reallocation yields

**`de^P/dη < 0 < de^C/dη`.**

Canonical condition (G):

`A_R ω_R'(η)D_R'(x) > -A_S ω_S'(η)D_S'(x)`.

## Welfare benchmark

Under the frozen quasilinear benchmark and stated parameter restrictions:

- `e^P<e^C<e^W`;
- `e^C-e^P` widens with `η`;
- `e^W-e^P` widens with `η`.

## Organizational implication

Conditional on a valid private retention threshold, a nonempty local under-retention region exists: private organization can drop the information-producing capacity while social welfare favors retention.

## Contribution boundary

The contribution candidate is the **reallocation-induced divergence in private and system information incentives**, not the existence of downstream information, user innovation, positive information externalities, or channel structure.
