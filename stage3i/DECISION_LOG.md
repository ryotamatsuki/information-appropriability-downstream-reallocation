# Stage 3-I — Decision Log

## Trigger

Stage 6-R terminated the previous static contribution because, after label stripping, its core game collapsed to established dual-channel costly-effort free-riding. The binding deficiency was absence of an independent information state/decision/response margin.

## Rule adopted

A Stage 3-I candidate may survive only when information is formally essential. In particular:

- replacing information by ordinary service effort must change the game;
- deleting the information object must remove an equilibrium-relevant arrow;
- Xu-type dual-channel free riding must not reproduce the headline result without adding the new information object;
- the new result must not be mechanically assumed.

## Candidate generation

Ten families A–J were generated before ranking. Ex-ante scoring weights were fixed before final candidate disposition.

## First kill round

A, B and I failed because their central logic can be reconstructed as generic dynamic/specific investment or is already strongly represented in vertical quality/learning-asset models.

C, D, E and F pass a narrow information-essentiality test but fail prior art: data ownership, portability, acquisition/disclosure and signal-precision acquisition already have direct solved literatures.

## Second kill round

G initially appeared promising because multiple downstream nodes can strategically produce information. The close-literature search showed that multiple private signals and strategic acquisition/sharing are already common. The only element that remained plausibly distinct was that nodes diagnose different dimensions of a product/problem state. G was therefore killed as a separate mechanism and that distinct object was isolated as H.

## Preferred-candidate decision

H — diagnostic/informational diversity — survives because its central object is the geometry of heterogeneous signals:

`P = Sigma_0^{-1} + sum_i tau_i h_i h_i'`.

The marginal value of source `i` is source-composition dependent:

`h_i' P^{-2} h_i`.

This cannot be represented as a scalar route value without deleting the multidimensional information problem.

The strongest Stage 3 threat is Xiong, Li & Lang (2025), supplemented by Myatt & Wallace (2019), Migrow & Squintani (2023), and Board & Meyer-ter-Vehn (2024). These papers prevent any claim of established novelty at Stage 3, but the inspected games do not reproduce the full proposed vertical reallocation/source-effort/posterior-design architecture.

Decision: **H = PREFERRED**.

## Runner-up decision

J — retention for learning option value — also passes information essentiality and Anti-Xu. It is not chosen because it simultaneously requires uncertainty, dynamics and a retention/stopping decision and faces a strong experimentation/endogenous-organization literature.

Decision: **J = TOP 3 / runner-up only**.

## No third candidate

A third candidate is not forced. G would duplicate H once signal heterogeneity is made essential; all other candidates have stronger prior-art kills. Stage 3 permits at most TOP 3 rather than requiring three.

## Canonical verdict

`GO`

Routing:

`GO TO STAGE 4 — MINIMAL MODEL`

This GO has narrow meaning: H is worth one minimal falsification attempt. It is not a claim that H is publication-ready or proposition-level novel.

## Authorized next modification

Stage 4 may introduce only the multidimensional diagnostic information structure and the minimum two-node effort/reallocation game required to test it.

Not authorized in Stage 4:

- dynamics;
- ownership/property rights;
- portability;
- disclosure;
- bargaining;
- contracts;
- endogenous entry;
- additional upstream firms;
- strategic wholesale pricing;
- foreclosure/RRC.

## Primary falsification rule

Switch diagnostic diversity off by imposing collinear/redundant signals, e.g. `h_1=h_2`.

If the headline concentration/retention result survives essentially unchanged, H is not information-specific enough and must be killed.
